SOURCE AND INTERPRETATION

Every scanner, analyst, entry, and exit rule on this page is our interpretation of a seb.ai Instagram reel that described a three-part setup (a coin scanner, six analysis bots, and a 24/7 terminal) but gave no indicators, timeframes, entry or exit rules, stops, or position sizing.

The reel’s marketing claims — including turning $1,000 into $101,607 in 10 days, and $14,911 from seven trades on day 10 after scanning 86 coins — are unverified. They are not used as targets or as evidence of an edge. The “comment GROKBOT for the guide” pattern is a common lead-in to paid funnels; this snapshot is not that guide.

A different public meaning of “Jev” (TypeSafe AI’s System One model) was also reviewed. It is not what the reel specified, and this snapshot does not implement or call that hosted model.

JEV DESIGN (OUR INTERPRETATION)

The system scans a universe of coins each 4-hour bar, ranks the top 3 names that clear a liquidity filter, and trades only when the six deterministic analyst modules reach a net consensus of ±3 or more. Modules are rule-based (not live language-model calls) so the backtest is reproducible. Shorts are allowed.

Six analyst modules

Trend

EMA 20 / 50 / SMA 200 stacked, with ADX above 20 required for a vote.

Momentum

RSI, 10-bar rate of change, and MACD histogram must agree (at least two of three).

Mean reversion

RSI under 30 or a close below the lower Bollinger band votes long; the opposite votes short.

Volume / flow proxy

A volume burst (1.5× the 20-bar average) plus a close near the high or low. This is a candle proxy, not true order flow.

Funding / derivatives

Fade crowded funding beyond ±0.03%. On Stellar this module votes 0 (no perpetual funding).

Volatility / regime

Votes with the trend only when the market is not in a volatility crisis (ATR vs its median) and ADX is not weak.

Entries, exits, and risk

A trade is opened only if the net vote is at least +3 (long) or −3 (short). Every trade has a mandatory stop at 2.0× ATR and a take-profit at 3.0× ATR. A position also exits on a consensus flip (net vote of ±2 against it) or after 48 bars. Default risk is 0.5% of equity per trade, with a hard cap of 1%, and a per-trade notional cap of $2,000 on a $10,000 paper book.

AUTONOMY AND SAFETY LIMITS

Paper is the default mode. A test-net path exists but stays unconnected until test keys are present. Real-money (live) mode is hard-locked until an explicit approval file is created and the operator confirms a starting amount. Deposits never turn live trading on by themselves. This public page has no login, no order buttons, and no kill-switch control.

  • Mandatory stop-loss on every trade (2× ATR by default)
  • Risk 0.5% per trade, hard cap 1%
  • Max $2,000 notional per trade; $3,000 per coin
  • Max 3 open positions; max 2× gross leverage
  • 2% daily loss cap (hard ceiling 3%)
  • Drawdown halt at 12% (hard ceiling 15%)
  • 80% of starting-balance hard stop (Stellar path)
  • Max 10 orders per minute
  • Stale-data halt (30s) and repeated-error halt
  • Heartbeat / dead-man switch (30s)
  • Kill switch: cancel open orders and flatten / convert back to XLM
  • Withdrawals (Stellar) only to one pre-approved public address

Thirty-three unit and fault-injection tests cover these mode locks, size caps, stops, daily-loss and drawdown halts, stale data, error rate, kill switch, and Stellar sweep-address rules. The trading-account key, if one is ever created, stays only on the trading machine and is never shown or sent. Seed phrases and private keys are never requested or accepted.

STELLAR ROUTE — LIVE LOCKED

Live Stellar trading is locked. The recommended path is a dedicated trading account that holds only the $50–$100 you choose to risk. Profits and leftovers can be swept back only to one public address you provide. Your main wallet’s seed phrase is never requested. Coinbase / Kraken remains an optional parallel deposit path, also approval-gated.

Trading uses Stellar’s built-in exchange and the official USDC/XLM liquidity pool (Circle issuer only). SDEX has no native stop-loss, so stops, the 2% daily cap, the drawdown halt, the 80% starting-balance hard stop, the order-rate cap, stale-data and error halts, and the kill switch are enforced by the engine. The kill switch cancels offers and converts leftover official USDC back to XLM.
